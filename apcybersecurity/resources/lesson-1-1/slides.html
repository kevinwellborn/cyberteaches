Big idea

Social engineering: hacking the human

Social engineering tricks a person into giving away information, money or access.

It is often easier to fool one person than to break strong technology.

Most data breaches involve a human mistake, like clicking a link or sharing a password.

The goal is usually

a password · a payment · a download · a door held open

Ask: Why would an attacker call a receptionist instead of hacking the server? Because the person may simply give the password away.
Know the attacks

Types of social engineering

PhishingA fake email or message from a "trusted" sender
Spear phishingPhishing aimed at one person, using their details
Smishing / vishingPhishing by text message / by phone call
PretextingA made-up story: "I'm new in IT and need..."
BaitingA free prize or a USB drive left in the parking lot
TailgatingFollowing someone through a locked door
Have students give an example of each from their own life (scam texts about packages are very common). Tailgating is physical: social engineering is not only online.
Why it works

Seven psychological levers

Urgency"Act in 1 hour!"
Authority"This is the principal."
Scarcity"Only 3 spots left."
Fear"Your account was hacked."
Curiosity"See who viewed your photo."
Helpfulness"Can you hold the door?"
Consensus"Everyone already signed up."
The trickMake you act before you think
These levers appear on AP-style questions. Many messages use more than one. Ask students which levers they feel most often.
Detect attacks

Anatomy of a phishing email

From: Help Desk <helpdesk@lincoln.edu> · Reply-To: support@lincoln-edu-help.com
Subject: URGENT: Your account will be locked
We detected unusual activity. Verify your account within 1 hour or it will be locked.
https://lincoln.edu.account-verify.net/login
Please enter your password to confirm it is you.

1Reply-To goes to another domain 2Urgency 3The real domain is account-verify.net 4Asks for a password

Read a link right to left: the real domain is the two parts just before the first single slash. lincoln.edu is just a subdomain of account-verify.net. Real IT never asks for your password.
Guided practice · think, pair, share

Name the lever

1. "This is Principal Johnson. Buy 5 gift cards for staff prizes and text me the codes." Authority

2. "Your package can't be delivered. Pay a $1.99 fee in 24 hours or it is returned." Urgency

3. "Only 10 free concert tickets left for students. Log in to claim yours." Scarcity

4. A person carrying heavy boxes asks you to swipe them into the building. Helpfulness · tailgating

Pairs agree on an answer before each reveal. Ask for evidence: "I think it is ___ because ___." Number 2 is smishing; number 4 is physical social engineering.
Mitigate risk

The defense: stop, verify, report

StopPressure is a warning sign. Slow down before you click, pay or share.
VerifyUse a separate, trusted channel: call the number you already have, or type the website yourself.
ReportTell IT or a trusted adult. Use the "Report phishing" button. Then delete it.

Never verify by replying or calling the number in the message. The attacker controls those.

This is the most tested idea: verification must happen outside the suspicious message. Also mention multi-factor authentication limits the damage if a password is stolen.

AP-style practice

Try one

An employee receives an email from a known vendor asking to update the bank account used for invoice payments. Which of the following is the best way to respond?

  1. Reply to the email and ask the vendor to confirm the change
  2. Call the vendor using the phone number already on file
  3. Update the account because the vendor's name is correct
  4. Forward the email to coworkers to see if it looks real

B. Verify through a separate, trusted channel. A reply goes straight back to the attacker.

Then students answer the four AP-style questions in Topic 1.1 online. Discuss why A is the most common wrong answer.

Before you go

Text message from +1 (555) 013-2209
Hi, it's your bank's fraud team. Your card was used in another state. Reply with your card number and PIN within 10 minutes to stop the charge.

1. Which lever is used?   2. Name one warning sign.   3. What should you do?
