11. Which of the following best explains why social engineering attacks are often successful?

  1. They exploit weaknesses in encryption algorithms.
  2. They exploit human trust and emotions to bypass technical controls.
  3. They require physical access to a server room.
  4. They only work on outdated operating systems.

12. An attacker researches a principal on social media, then sends her an email that mentions her recent conference trip and includes a malicious attachment. Which of the following best describes this attack?

  1. Tailgating
  2. Baiting
  3. Spear phishing

13. Which of the following controls would best reduce the impact if an employee enters a password on a phishing page?

  1. Requiring multi-factor authentication for logins
  2. Requiring users to change passwords every week
  3. Blocking all email attachments
  4. Increasing the size of the email inbox

  1. Ban personal phones on campus
  2. Train staff and students to recognize and report phishing, and practice with simulated phishing emails

Part B

6. Any three: the Reply-To is a different domain (wfs-hr-portal.com); urgency ("by 5:00 p.m. today", "pay delayed"); the link's real domain is not the school's; it asks for bank or login details by email; generic, unexpected request about money.

7. wfs-hr-portal.com. "westfield-schools.org" is only a subdomain at the start of the link. The attacker could steal her login and bank details and redirect her paycheck (payroll diversion).

Part C

8. Do not click. Contact payroll or HR using the phone number or office she already knows, or the official staff portal typed in herself. Report the email to IT.

9. Do not send money. Contact the friend another way (call them or talk in person); the account may be hacked. "Don't tell anyone" is a warning sign.

10. Do not unlock the door. Ask the worker to check in at the main office and call the staff member they are delivering to. This prevents tailgating.

Part D

11. B · 12. C (targeted with personal details) · 13. A (MFA stops a stolen password from being enough) · 14. B

Reflect: the attacker controls the message, so a reply or a phone number in it reaches the attacker. A separate channel you already trust reaches the real person.
