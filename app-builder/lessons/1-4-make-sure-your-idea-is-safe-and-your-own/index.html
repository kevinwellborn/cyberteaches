Make sure your idea is safe and your own

Before you fall in love with an idea, check three things. Is it respectful to everyone who might see it? Is it your own work? Does it leave other people's creations and private information alone unless you have the right to use them? A few minutes of checking now can save your whole project later.

Images, music, characters, and code that someone else made belong to them. You can use them only when the owner allows it, for example through a free-to-use license. When in doubt, make your own or pick material that is clearly free to use.

Real developers use open-source libraries and AI tools all the time, and you can too. The honest way is to say what you used, and to understand every part of your own code. Start a list today of anything you use that you did not make yourself.

Idea check

Respectful and decent for all ages?  Yes
My own idea and my own work?  Yes
Uses only images and sounds I made or may use?  Yes, I drew the icons
Collects private information?  Only a first name, saved on the device
Credits list started?  Yes

Do this

  1. Read your purpose sentence and ask: would I be comfortable showing this app to my principal and my grandparents?
  2. List any images, music, characters, or data you planned to use. For each, ask: did I make it, or do I have the right to use it?
  3. Replace anything you do not have the right to use with your own work or with free-to-use material.
  4. If your app stores information about people, write down what it stores and why. Store as little as possible.
  5. Start a credits list in the second box below. Write down every library, tool, and AI assistant as you use them.
  6. Answer the five idea check questions for your own app in the first box below, one on each line, like the example.

With your class: Okay or not okay?

About 15 minutes. Working on your own? Do these steps with a friend or someone at home.

  1. Get into a small group. For each case, everyone votes thumbs up (okay) or thumbs down (not okay) at the same time. Then one person says why.
  2. Case A: A student uses a hit song by a famous singer as the background music in their app.
  3. Case B: A student uses a free-to-use icon set and lists it on their credits page.
  4. Case C: A homework list app asks every user for their full name, home address, and birthday.
  5. Case D: A student has an AI tool write the whole app and cannot explain how the code works.
  6. Check your votes. A is not okay: the song belongs to someone else. B is okay: free to use, with credit. C is not okay: it collects much more than it needs. D is not okay: AI can help, but the work and the understanding must be yours.
  7. Now trade with a partner. Read each other's purpose sentence and idea check. Look for one thing that could be a problem, and tell them kindly.
  8. Write what your partner found in the last box below.

For the Congressional App Challenge: The rules require apps to be decent and respectful and to respect intellectual property and privacy. Libraries, tools, and AI are allowed only if you clearly document them, AI cannot be the whole build, and you must understand your own code.

Make sure your idea is safe and your own vocabulary

  • Intellectual property (IP): Creations that belong to someone, such as images, music, characters, code, and brand names.
  • Open source: Code that its authors let others use, usually with a license that says how.
  • License: The rules for how you may use someone else's work.
  • Privacy: People's right to control their personal information.
  • Disclosure: Telling people clearly what tools, libraries, and AI help you used.

Quick check questions

Try each one, then open the answer. To save your progress, take this quiz in the interactive course.

1. A student wants to use a famous cartoon character as their app's mascot. What is the problem?

  1. (A) Cartoons are not allowed in apps
  2. (B) The character is someone else's intellectual property
  3. (C) Mascots slow the app down
  4. (D) There is no problem
Show answer

(B) The character is someone else's intellectual property You need the owner's permission to use their character. Draw your own mascot instead.

2. Which is the most honest way to use an AI tool on your project?

  1. (A) Have it write the whole app and say you wrote it
  2. (B) Use it to help you learn or fix a bug, write down that you used it, and make sure you understand the code
  3. (C) Use it secretly so nobody asks questions
  4. (D) Never tell anyone, because everyone uses AI
Show answer

(B) Use it to help you learn or fix a bug, write down that you used it, and make sure you understand the code Use AI as a helper, say that you used it, and be able to explain every part of your own code.

3. You used an open-source charting library. What must you do?

  1. (A) Hide it so the app looks more original
  2. (B) Clearly document that you used it
  3. (C) Rewrite it from scratch
  4. (D) Nothing
Show answer

(B) Clearly document that you used it Using a library is fine. Giving credit for it is the honest thing to do, and many licenses require it.
