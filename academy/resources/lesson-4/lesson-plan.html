Lesson 4: Repeat until

Time 50 minutes
Unit Loops
Puzzles 4.1 to 4.10

Overview

Students learn a second kind of loop: repeat until at flag, which keeps repeating until a condition is true. They compare it with the counting repeat, combine both in one program, and learn to recognize and fix infinite loops.

Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. Explain the difference between a counting loop and a repeat-until loop.
  2. Use repeat until at flag to reach a flag at an unknown distance.
  3. Combine a counting repeat and a repeat-until loop in one program.
  4. Identify and fix an infinite loop.

Materials

  • Projector and the Lesson 4 slides

  • Student devices with a web browser: cyberteaches.com/academy
  • A class on the Teacher Dashboard, with student logins or Google sign-in

Before class

  • Try puzzles 4.1 to 4.10 yourself (the answer key is on the Teacher Dashboard).
  • Pick a wall in the room for the warm-up.

Lesson procedure

TimeStageTeacher and student activity
5 minWarm-up
Walk until the wall Slide 3		Two students start at different distances from a wall. Compare "take 6 steps" with "walk until you touch the wall". Only the second works for both.
10 minDirect instruction
Slides 4 to 6		Introduce the repeat until at flag block with puzzle 4.1. Show an infinite loop (only turning) and ask what is missing. Model "count, then until" with puzzle 4.3.
5 minGuided practice
Think, pair, share Slides 7 to 8		Solve puzzle 4.2 together, then pairs find one step of the stairs in puzzle 4.4. Review the debugging tips.
12 minUnplugged practice
Classwork Slide 10		Students complete Parts A to C and E in pairs. Part C asks them to choose and justify a loop type. Part D is for early finishers.
15 minIndependent practice
Code Slide 11		Students solve puzzles 4.1 to 4.10. Watch for "stuck in a loop" messages and ask students what is missing inside the loop.
3 minClosure
Exit ticket Slide 12		Exit ticket: explain why repeat until at flag is better than repeat 9 times when the distance is unknown.

Assessment

  • Formative: classwork Parts A to C, observation during coding, and the exit ticket.
  • Progress: the Teacher Dashboard shows stars per puzzle and a Lesson 4 score out of 100 (3 stars = 100, 2 stars = 90, 1 star = 80 per puzzle).
  • Success criteria: the student solves at least 7 of 10 puzzles and can explain when to use repeat until instead of repeat.

Common misconceptions

  • Thinking repeat until checks only at the start, not before every repeat.
  • Putting only a turn inside the loop, causing an infinite loop.
  • Using repeat until for a part with no flag at the end.

Differentiation

  • Support: act out "until" with real movement; let students write a counting repeat first, then swap it.
  • Extension: solve puzzle 4.9 (back and forth) and 4.10 for 3 stars; design a puzzle where the flag is far away.
  • Accessibility: blocks can be added by tapping or by dragging, and moved with the ▲▼ buttons.

Supports for English learners

  • Visuals: post the block pictures and vocabulary on a word wall; point to blocks while you say them.
  • Gestures: students stand and turn left or right with the rover before coding turns.
  • Partners: pair students to read programs aloud; first-language talk is welcome.
  • Sentence frames: “The rover moves forward ___ it reaches the flag.” “A loop that never stops is an ___ loop.” “I use repeat until because I don't know ___ times.”
  • Classwork Part E uses a word bank, pictures and fill-in sentences.

Connections

  • Science: heat the water until it boils; a thermostat runs until a temperature is reached.
  • Everyday life: "keep stirring until it is smooth."
