Lesson 2: Repeat practice

Time 50 minutes
Unit Loops
Puzzles 2.1 to 2.10

Overview

A practice lesson with no new blocks. Students strengthen their understanding of the repeat loop by counting crystals and steps, choosing the right repeat number, and adding a single turn before or between repeats.

Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. Count crystals or steps to choose the number in a repeat block.
  2. Use one repeat block to collect a row or column of crystals.
  3. Place a turn before or between repeat blocks.
  4. Explain why a shorter program earns more stars.

Materials

  • Projector and the Lesson 2 slides
  • Printed Lesson 2 classwork, one per student (answer key for the teacher)
  • Student devices with a web browser: cyberteaches.com/academy
  • A class on the Teacher Dashboard, with student logins or Google sign-in

Before class

  • Try puzzles 2.1 to 2.10 yourself (the answer key is on the Teacher Dashboard).
  • Prepare a clapping or movement pattern for the warm-up.

Lesson procedure

TimeStageTeacher and student activity
5 minWarm-up
Clap the pattern Slide 3		Clap a pattern (clap, clap, stop) three times. Students copy it, then say it in one sentence: "repeat 3 times: clap, clap, stop."
10 minDirect instruction
Slides 4 to 6		Review the repeat block. Model counting crystals with your finger before setting the repeat number (5 crystals = repeat 5 [ F C ]). Show a turn that happens once, before the repeat (puzzle 2.6), and the crystals that are 3 squares apart (puzzle 2.7).
5 minGuided practice
Think, pair, share Slides 7 to 8		Solve puzzle 2.1 together, then pairs find the F F C pattern in puzzle 2.5 before the reveal. Review the debugging tips.
12 minUnplugged practice
Classwork Slide 10		Students complete Parts A to C and E in pairs. Part D is for early finishers. Listen for students counting aloud.
15 minIndependent practice
Code Slide 11		Students solve puzzles 2.1 to 2.10. Encourage counting before building. Monitor progress on the Teacher Dashboard.
3 minClosure
Exit ticket Slide 12		Exit ticket: six crystals in a row. Answer: repeat 6 [ F C ], 3 blocks.

Assessment

  • Formative: classwork Parts A to C, observation during coding, and the exit ticket.
  • Progress: the Teacher Dashboard shows stars per puzzle and a Lesson 2 score out of 100 (3 stars = 100, 2 stars = 90, 1 star = 80 per puzzle).
  • Success criteria: the student solves at least 8 of 10 puzzles and chooses the correct repeat number by counting.

Common misconceptions

  • Choosing the number of blocks instead of the number of crystals.
  • Putting the turn inside the repeat so it happens every time.
  • Forgetting that moving onto empty squares also needs F blocks.

Differentiation

  • Support: give students counters or a finger-tracking strategy; let them write the long program first.
  • Extension: solve puzzles 2.8 and 2.10 (a turn between two repeats) and compare programs with a partner.
  • Accessibility: blocks can be added by tapping or by dragging, and moved with the ▲▼ buttons.

Supports for English learners

  • Visuals: post the block pictures and vocabulary on a word wall; point to blocks while you say them.
  • Gestures: students stand and turn left or right with the rover before coding turns.
  • Partners: pair students to read programs aloud; first-language talk is welcome.
  • Sentence frames: “There are ___ crystals, so I repeat ___ times.” “The rover turns ___ first.” “The blocks ___ the repeat run again and again.”
  • Classwork Part E uses a word bank, pictures and fill-in sentences.

Connections

  • Math: counting and multiplication (7 × 2 blocks = 14 blocks without a repeat).
  • Music: repeated beats and choruses in songs.
