Lesson 3: Repeat patterns

Time 50 minutes
Unit Loops
Puzzles 3.1 to 3.10

Overview

Students find repeating patterns that include moves, turns and collects, build one copy of the pattern, and repeat it. They are introduced to nested loops: a repeat inside a repeat.

Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. Identify one copy of a repeating pattern that includes turns.
  2. Write a program that repeats a multi-step pattern.
  3. Use a nested loop (a repeat inside a repeat) to solve a shape puzzle.
  4. Compare the length of programs with and without loops.

Materials

  • Projector and the Lesson 3 slides
  • Printed Lesson 3 classwork, one per student (answer key for the teacher)
  • Student devices with a web browser: cyberteaches.com/academy
  • A class on the Teacher Dashboard, with student logins or Google sign-in

Before class

  • Try puzzles 3.1 to 3.10 yourself (the answer key is on the Teacher Dashboard).
  • Print or draw a colored-square pattern for the warm-up.

Lesson procedure

TimeStageTeacher and student activity
5 minWarm-up
What comes next? Slide 3		Show a color pattern (red, blue, blue, …). Students predict what comes next and identify one copy of the pattern.
10 minDirect instruction
Slides 4 to 5		Show a pattern with a turn in it using puzzle 3.2 (little steps): trace one step with your finger, then repeat it. Introduce a repeat inside a repeat with puzzle 3.4 (small square): the inner repeat does one side, the outer repeat does all four sides.
5 minGuided practice
Think, pair, share Slides 6 to 7		Solve puzzle 3.1 together (pattern F C F), then pairs work on puzzle 3.4 before the reveal. Review the debugging tips.
12 minUnplugged practice
Classwork Slide 9		Students complete Parts A to C and E in pairs. Part D is for early finishers. Check that students box exactly one copy of the pattern in Part C.
15 minIndependent practice
Code Slide 10		Students solve puzzles 3.1 to 3.10. Puzzles 3.4, 3.8 and 3.9 use nested loops. Monitor the dashboard.
3 minClosure
Exit ticket Slide 11		Exit ticket: explain repeat 4 [ repeat 3 [ F ] R ] (drives around a square, 5 blocks).

Assessment

  • Formative: classwork Parts A to C, observation during coding, and the exit ticket.
  • Progress: the Teacher Dashboard shows stars per puzzle and a Lesson 3 score out of 100 (3 stars = 100, 2 stars = 90, 1 star = 80 per puzzle).
  • Success criteria: the student solves at least 7 of 10 puzzles and can explain what the inner and outer loop each do.

Common misconceptions

  • Choosing a "copy" that is too long or too short, so the pattern does not line up.
  • Putting the turn inside the inner loop instead of the outer loop.
  • Counting blocks without counting the repeat blocks themselves.

Differentiation

  • Support: give students highlighters to mark one copy of the pattern; start with the long program first.
  • Extension: solve puzzles 3.8 (rectangle) and 3.10 (climbing stairs) and explain the two numbers of a nested repeat to a partner.
  • Accessibility: blocks can be added by tapping or by dragging, and moved with the ▲▼ buttons.

Supports for English learners

  • Visuals: post the block pictures and vocabulary on a word wall; point to blocks while you say them.
  • Gestures: students stand and turn left or right with the rover before coding turns.
  • Partners: pair students to read programs aloud; first-language talk is welcome.
  • Sentence frames: “One copy of the pattern is ___.” “A loop inside another loop is a ___ loop.” “The inner loop does one ___. The outer loop does all of them.”
  • Classwork Part E uses a word bank, pictures and fill-in sentences.

Connections

  • Math: shapes and perimeter (4 sides × 3 steps).
  • Art: tiling and repeating designs.
