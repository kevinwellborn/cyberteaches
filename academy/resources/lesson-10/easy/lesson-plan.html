Lesson 10: Tank missions

Time 50 minutes
Unit Loops and conditionals
Puzzles 10.1 to 10.10

Overview

Tank missions add two limits: a limited number of shells and a maximum number of blocks. There are more tanks than the limit allows one block each, so students must find patterns and use repeat, including nested repeats.

Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. Plan a program that fits inside a block limit.
  2. Use a repeat or nested repeat to perform many actions with few blocks.
  3. Use limited shells without missing.
  4. Explain how stars are earned inside a block limit.

Assessment

  • Formative: classwork Parts A to C, observation during coding, and the exit ticket.
  • Progress: the Teacher Dashboard shows stars per puzzle and a Lesson 10 score out of 100 (3 stars = 100, 2 stars = 90, 1 star = 80 per puzzle).
  • Success criteria: the student solves at least 7 of 10 missions inside the block limit.

Common misconceptions

  • Thinking a repeat counts as zero blocks (it counts as one, plus the blocks inside).
  • Firing at squares with no tank and running out of shells.
  • Not noticing the limit until the editor stops adding blocks.

Differentiation

  • Support: have students write the long program on paper first, then box the pattern.
  • Extension: 3 stars on every mission; explain the shortest solution for the final mission.
  • Accessibility: blocks can be added by tapping or by dragging, and moved with the ▲▼ buttons.

Supports for English learners

  • Visuals: post the block pictures and vocabulary on a word wall; point to blocks while you say them.
  • Gestures: students stand and turn left or right with the rover before coding turns.
  • Partners: pair students to read programs aloud; first-language talk is welcome.
  • Sentence frames: “The limit is ___ blocks, so I use a repeat.” “My program is ___ because it uses fewer blocks.” “I fire only ___ a tank is ahead.”
  • Classwork Part E uses a word bank, pictures and fill-in sentences.

Connections

  • Math: multiplication and grouping (2 × 4 tanks).
  • Engineering: designing within limits (budget, materials).
