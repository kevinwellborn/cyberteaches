Lesson 9: Tank patrols

Time 50 minutes
Unit Loops and conditionals
Puzzles 9.1 to 9.10

Overview

A practice lesson with no new blocks. Students write patrol programs that move along a road, look to the side, fire only when a tank is present, and turn back, repeating until no tanks are left.

Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. Write a patrol pattern: move, look, fire if a tank is ahead, turn back.
  2. Use repeat until no tanks left instead of counting squares.
  3. Patrol both sides of a road.
  4. Debug patrols that forget to turn back or look the wrong way.

Assessment

  • Formative: classwork Parts A to C, observation during coding, and the exit ticket.
  • Progress: the Teacher Dashboard shows stars per puzzle and a Lesson 9 score out of 100 (3 stars = 100, 2 stars = 90, 1 star = 80 per puzzle).
  • Success criteria: the student solves at least 7 of 10 puzzles and fixes at least 3 of the 4 patrols in Part C.

Common misconceptions

  • Forgetting to turn back after looking.
  • Looking the wrong way (left instead of right) for tanks below the road.
  • Firing without if tank ahead and running out of shells.

Differentiation

  • Support: act out the patrol with a student walking along a row of desks.
  • Extension: 3 stars on 9.6, 9.8 and 9.10; design a two-sided patrol.
  • Accessibility: blocks can be added by tapping or by dragging, and moved with the ▲▼ buttons.

Supports for English learners

  • Visuals: post the block pictures and vocabulary on a word wall; point to blocks while you say them.
  • Gestures: students stand and turn left or right with the rover before coding turns.
  • Partners: pair students to read programs aloud; first-language talk is welcome.
  • Sentence frames: “At every square, the rover looks. If a tank is there, ___ it fires.” “After it looks up, the rover turns ___.” “The patrol stops when no tanks are ___.”
  • Classwork Part E uses a word bank, pictures and fill-in sentences.

Connections

  • Careers: security guards, inspectors and robots that patrol warehouses.
  • Science: scanning an area step by step.
