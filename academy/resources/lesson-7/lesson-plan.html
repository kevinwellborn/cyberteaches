Lesson 7: Tank battle

Time 50 minutes
Unit Loops and conditionals
Puzzles 7.1 to 7.10

Overview

Students first drive the rover with the keyboard (drive mode) and see their moves become a program. They then learn three new blocks: fire cannon, if tank ahead (their first conditional), and repeat until no tanks left, and write patrol programs that destroy tanks automatically.

Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. Explain that each move in drive mode becomes a block in a program.
  2. Use fire cannon to destroy tanks in the rover's line of fire.
  3. Use if tank ahead so the rover fires only when a tank is present.
  4. Use repeat until no tanks left to patrol until every tank is destroyed.

Lesson procedure

TimeStageTeacher and student activity
5 minWarm-up
Look before you act Slide 3		Students share everyday if-rules ("If it is raining, take an umbrella"). Introduce the idea that an if only runs when something is true.
10 minDirect instruction
Slides 4 to 7		Demo drive mode (puzzle 7.1). Introduce fire cannon (puzzle 7.2), if tank ahead (puzzle 7.5) and repeat until no tanks left. Explain limited shells and the red ! warning.
5 minGuided practice
Think, pair, share Slides 8 to 9		Solve puzzle 7.3 together, then pairs solve puzzle 7.9 (unknown road) before the reveal. Review the debugging tips.
12 minUnplugged practice
Classwork Slide 11		Students complete Parts A to C and E in pairs. Part D is for early finishers.
15 minIndependent practice
Code Slide 12		Students solve puzzles 7.1 to 7.10. Puzzle 7.1 is drive mode; the rest are programs.
3 minClosure
Exit ticket Slide 13		Exit ticket: why use if tank ahead before fire cannon in a patrol? (So shells are not wasted.)

Assessment

  • Formative: classwork Parts A to C, observation during coding, and the exit ticket.
  • Progress: the Teacher Dashboard shows stars per puzzle and a Lesson 7 score out of 100 (3 stars = 100, 2 stars = 90, 1 star = 80 per puzzle).
  • Success criteria: the student solves at least 7 of 10 puzzles and can explain what if tank ahead does.

Common misconceptions

  • Firing before turning to face the tank.
  • Thinking a shell can turn corners.
  • Staying in a tank's line of fire after the red ! appears.
  • Firing at every square when tanks are only at some squares.

Differentiation

  • Support: let students play drive mode again to plan moves; give them the patrol pattern and let them add the if.
  • Extension: 3 stars on 7.6 and 7.10; design a patrol puzzle with tanks on both sides.
  • Accessibility: blocks can be added by tapping or by dragging, and moved with the ▲▼ buttons.

Supports for English learners

  • Visuals: post the block pictures and vocabulary on a word wall; point to blocks while you say them.
  • Gestures: students stand and turn left or right with the rover before coding turns.
  • Partners: pair students to read programs aloud; first-language talk is welcome.
  • Sentence frames: “If a tank is ahead, then the rover ___.” “The shell flies ___ until it hits a tank or a rock.” “The loop stops when no ___ are left.”
  • Classwork Part E uses a word bank, pictures and fill-in sentences.

Connections

  • Everyday life: if-rules like traffic lights and weather.
  • Science: sensors that react only when they detect something.
