Lesson 1: Repeat and collect

Time 50 minutes
Unit Loops
Puzzles 1.1 to 1.10

Overview

Students program a rover to drive and collect crystals using a sequence of blocks. They discover that long programs often contain a repeating pattern, and learn to use a repeat loop to make programs shorter, easier to read and easier to fix.

Learning objectives

Students will be able to:

  1. Explain that a program is a sequence of instructions a computer follows in order.
  2. Write a sequence of blocks that moves the rover and collects crystals.
  3. Identify a repeating pattern in a program.
  4. Use a repeat loop (including a loop inside a loop) to shorten a program.
  5. Debug a program by running it, watching the result, and changing one thing at a time.

Materials

Before class

  • Try puzzles 1.1 to 1.10 yourself (the answer key is on the Teacher Dashboard).
  • Create the class on the Teacher Dashboard and share the code, or print student login cards.
  • Clear a short path in the room for the warm-up.

Lesson procedure

TimeStageTeacher and student activity
5 minWarm-up
Be the robot Slide 3		A student "programmer" guides a "robot" partner to the door using only step forward, turn left, turn right. Count how many times "step forward" was said. Ask: Is there a shorter way to say it?
10 minDirect instruction
Slides 4 to 10		Define program and sequence. Introduce the map and blocks; stress that left and right are from the rover's point of view. Solve puzzle 1.1 together. Show the 8-block program for puzzle 1.2, find the pattern, and replace it with repeat 4 [ F C ] (3 blocks). Explain the block goal and stars.
5 minGuided practice
Think, pair, share Slides 11 to 12		Pairs find the pattern in puzzle 1.3 before the reveal: repeat 3 [ F F C ]. Review the debugging tips.
12 minUnplugged practice
Classwork Slide 13		Students complete Parts A to C and E in pairs. Part D (design your own) is for early finishers. Check Part B question 4 (the left/right turn).
15 minIndependent practice
Code Slide 14		Students sign in and solve puzzles 1.1 to 1.10. Encourage "make it work, then make it shorter" for 3 stars. Monitor stars live on the Teacher Dashboard.
3 minClosure
Exit ticket Slide 15		Students rewrite F C F C F C F C F C with a repeat and count the blocks (answer: repeat 5 [ F C ], 3 blocks).

Assessment

  • Formative: classwork Parts A to C, observation during coding, and the exit ticket.
  • Progress: the Teacher Dashboard shows stars per puzzle and a Lesson 1 score out of 100 (3 stars = 100, 2 stars = 90, 1 star = 80 per puzzle).
  • Success criteria: the student solves at least 7 of 10 puzzles and can rewrite a long program with a repeat.

Common misconceptions

  • Turning left or right from the screen's view instead of the rover's.
  • Setting the repeat number without counting the crystals first.
  • Putting a block outside the repeat when it belongs inside (or the reverse).
  • Thinking a turn also moves the rover. It only turns on the spot.

Differentiation

  • Support: pair students; let them build the long program first, then find the pattern. Lesson 2 (Repeat practice) gives more easy puzzles.
  • Extension: aim for 3 stars on every puzzle; try puzzles 1.6 and 1.10 (a repeat inside a repeat); swap Part D designs with a partner.
  • Accessibility: blocks can be added by tapping or by dragging, and moved with the ▲▼ buttons.

Supports for English learners

  • Visuals: post the block pictures and vocabulary on a word wall; point to blocks while you say them.
  • Gestures: students stand and turn left or right with the rover before coding turns.
  • Partners: pair students to read programs aloud; first-language talk is welcome.
  • Sentence frames: “First, the rover moves ___. Then it ___ the crystal.” “The same steps happen again and again. This is a ___.” “I use a ___ block to make my program shorter.”
  • Classwork Part E uses a word bank, pictures and fill-in sentences.

Connections

  • Math: multiplication as repeated addition (4 × [F C] = 8 blocks).
  • Everyday life: recipes, dance moves and exercise sets all repeat steps.
