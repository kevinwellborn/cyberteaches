Lab 2 Teacher Key: Computer Lab Disconnected

Root causes, solutions, and verification results for tickets 2041–2046, plus the documentation key and a sample security and segmentation plan.

Setup and pacing

  • Day 1: Parts 1–2 (tickets). Day 2: finish Part 2, Part 3 (documentation). Day 3: Part 4 (security, AI evaluation). Day 4: Part 5 (Packet Tracer) and report.
  • Give out the verification results below only after a team states its solution and its verification test.
  • Triage order: a strong answer works 2046 first (four stations down), then the single-station outages, then 2045 (slow but working). Accept other orders with a reason.

Documentation key (Part 3)

  • Solid lines: router to both switches, switches to stations, file server, and printer. Dashed lines: teacher laptop, projector, thermostat, and visitor laptops to the router's Wi-Fi.
  • Static: ROUTER-204 (10.204.0.1), FILESERVER-204 (10.204.0.10), PRINTER-204 (10.204.0.20). Dynamic (DHCP .100–.199): stations, teacher laptop, projector, thermostat, visitors.
  • Why static for the printer and server: every station must find them at a constant address; DHCP fits the stations and mobile devices, which do not need a fixed address and are easier to manage automatically.
  • Public versus private: the WAN address 198.51.100.40 is public; every 10.204.0.x address is private.

Sample segmentation plan

  • Teacher and servers (high security): teacher laptop, file server, printer access
  • Student stations: stations 01–16; allowed to reach the file server and printer only
  • IoT: smart projector, smart thermostat; no access to the other segments
  • Guest wireless: visitor laptops; internet only
  • Firewall role: rules between segments allow only the needed services (file sharing, printing) and block the rest, so a compromised IoT or guest device cannot move laterally to the file server.

What to look for in the AI evaluation

Typical AI replies recommend enterprise firewalls, RADIUS servers, or separate physical networks for each group. Credit students who call these overcomplicated or costly for one classroom, who notice when the AI forgets the guest speakers or the IoT devices, and who check that any addressing it proposes is valid.

Packet Tracer check (Part 5)

Expected: stations lease .100–.199 (show ip dhcp binding on the router), the server and printer are static, the teacher laptop joins the WPA2-PSK SSID, and pings to 10.204.0.1 and 10.204.0.10 succeed. A station configured for DHCP before the router has a pool will show an APIPA address (Packet Tracer reports "DHCP request failed. APIPA is being used"), which recreates ticket 2042.
