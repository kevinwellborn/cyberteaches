Lab 3 Teacher Key: Game Day Network Design

One model design that meets every requirement in the event brief. Many designs are valid; grade on whether the team's choices are correct, consistent, and justified.

Setup and pacing

  • Day 1: Parts 1–2. Day 2: Part 3 (subnetting; budget extra time). Day 3: Part 4. Days 4–5: Packet Tracer build, verification, and the document.
  • The main traps are the gateway address in each host count, the concrete wall (separate WAPs for the lobby), the uplink ports, and rule 4 (coaches and judges use the same server on different ports).

  • WAPs: bleachers 180 ÷ 40 = 4.5 → 5; lobby (behind the concrete wall) 70 ÷ 40 = 1.75 → 2; one more WAP on the stage for the 9 staff and coach laptops and 6 judge tablets (9 of 20 laptops plus 6 of 40 mobile devices fits one WAP). Model total: 8. Accept 7 if the team justifies putting the internal SSIDs on the gym WAPs and shows the capacity still works.
  • Wired ports: 20 + 1 + 2 + 3 + 1 = 27 endpoints, plus 8 WAPs = 35 ports.
  • Switches: switch 1 loses one port to the router and one to switch 2 (22 left); switch 2 loses its uplink (23 left). 22 + 23 = 45 ≥ 35, so 2 switches minimum. A third switch in the booth is fine if justified by cabling convenience; every run is under 100 m.

Part 2 key: AI draft evaluation

ItemJudgment
1 Full mesh of every switch and WAPOvercomplicated and costly; mesh needs more cabling, devices, and management. A star or hybrid (with a redundant link for the stream) fits a one-day event.
2 Two WAPs; players on Wi-FiWrong twice: two WAPs cannot serve 250 phones (at least 7 by capacity and the wall), and player PCs on Wi-Fi violate rule 1 and the latency need.
3 One SSID, password announcedMisses requirements: no segmentation, so spectators share a network with judges and the server (violates rules 2 and 4); announcing the password defeats it.
4 SubnetsTechnically wrong: 10.60.0.0/26 (.0–.63) overlaps 10.60.0.32/27 (.32–.63). Also oversized (players need a /27), staff /25 is wasteful, and judges, coaches, and spectators are missing.
5 NGFW at perimeter, allow all insideThe perimeter firewall is reasonable, but allowing all internal traffic removes the segmentation that enforces rule 4 and allows lateral movement.

Improved prompt should name the user groups and counts, the latency and reliability needs, the director's four rules, the venue layout and wall, the equipment limits (24-port switches, 40 devices per WAP), and the address blocks.

Part 4 key: wireless and firewall rules

  • SSIDs: Invitational-Guest (VLAN 60; WPA2 or WPA3 with a strong password posted at the entrance; captive portal with terms of use; 3-hour session limit; no access to internal networks; optional device limit), plus internal SSIDs for judges (VLAN 30), coaches (VLAN 40), and staff (VLAN 50), each with WPA3 and a strong, unique password. Why the order matters: rule 105 must sit above 106–107 because ANY includes the event network. Coaches reach only port 443 on the server, so the scoring app on 8443 and the judges' subnet are denied by rule 112 (rule 4). Only the management workstation gets SSH, and Telnet is never allowed (rule 3). Players get the game server only (no internet), as the brief says.

Part 5 check

  • show vlan brief lists each access port under the right VLAN; the trunk port does not appear in the list (trunks are not shown as VLAN members there).
  • Pings within a VLAN succeed. With router subinterfaces, pings between VLANs succeed until the ACL is applied; after ip access-group 110 in on the spectator subinterface, the smartphone can no longer reach 10.60.0.x.
