Section II scoring guidelines: Brightwater Public Library (11 points)

(a) Using Sources 1 and 2, explain why the circulation desk computer cannot reach the staff file server. Then describe the switch configuration change that would fix the problem, and describe how you would verify the fix. (3 pt; LO 4.5.D, 3.6.B)
  • 1 point: Explains that port Gi0/9 is assigned to VLAN 20 (PUBLIC), so the circulation PC received a 192.168.20.x address on the public segment and cannot reach VLAN 10 devices, because devices on different VLANs cannot communicate unless routing is configured.
  • 1 point: Describes configuring Gi0/9 as an access port assigned to VLAN 10 (STAFF), so the PC joins the staff segment (and then renews its IP address).
  • 1 point: Describes a verification, such as running show vlan brief to confirm Gi0/9 is listed under VLAN 10 and pinging the staff file server at 192.168.10.8 from the circulation PC.
Sample response. Source 1 shows that Gi0/9, the circulation desk port, is in VLAN 20 PUBLIC, and Source 2 confirms the PC has the public address 192.168.20.104. Devices on different VLANs cannot communicate unless routing is configured, so it cannot reach the staff server on VLAN 10. I would configure Gi0/9 as an access port in VLAN 10 and have the PC renew its address. To verify, I would run show vlan brief to confirm Gi0/9 is now listed under VLAN 10 and then ping 192.168.10.8 from the circulation PC.
(b) Identify the most likely root cause of the patron laptop's problem, citing evidence from Source 2. Then determine one solution, and justify why it would resolve the problem. (2 pt; LO 3.6.B, 3.6.C)
  • 1 point: Identifies an exhausted DHCP pool as the root cause, citing the APIPA address 169.254.88.3 and 20 of 20 addresses leased.
  • 1 point: Determines an appropriate solution, such as expanding the DHCP pool range or shortening the 7-day lease, and justifies it (for example, about 60 devices a day but only 20 addresses; one-time visitors keep addresses for 7 days, so a shorter lease recycles them quickly).
Sample response. The DHCP pool is exhausted: the laptop has the APIPA address 169.254.88.3, and all 20 of 20 addresses are leased. About 60 devices a day connect but most visit only weekly, so the 7-day lease keeps addresses tied up by devices that have left. Shortening the lease to a few hours would recycle those addresses, and expanding the range (for example, to .100–.250) would also give enough addresses for a day's patrons.
(c) Identify two indicators of a security threat in Source 3. Then describe one control, consistent with policy L2, that would limit the impact of this activity, and explain how it works. (2 pt; LO 4.5.C, 3.4.B)
  • 1 point: Identifies two different threat indicators, such as the unauthorized port scan of the staff segment, repeated failed SSH logins (35 and 22 attempts), the connection to a known malicious IP, or activity outside business hours (after the 8:00 p.m. closing).
  • 1 point: Describes a control consistent with L2, such as a deny-by-default firewall or access rules between VLAN 20 and VLAN 10 that permit only printing traffic, and explains that it blocks public devices from reaching staff systems and limits lateral movement.
Sample response. Two indicators are the port scan of 192.168.10.0/24 at 21:47, which occurred after the library closed at 8:00 p.m., and the 35 failed SSH logins to the staff file server at 21:52. Under policy L2, I would add deny-by-default access rules between VLAN 20 and VLAN 10 that permit only printing traffic. A public device could then not even reach the staff server's SSH port, which stops lateral movement from the public segment.
(d) Describe how you would configure the patrons' guest wireless network so that it meets policy L3 and policies L1 and L2. (2 pt; LO 3.3.D, 1.4.C)
  • 1 point: Describes creating a separate guest SSID with a strong password and WPA2 or WPA3 encryption, and disabling the guest network's access to the internal network (staff systems).
  • 1 point: Describes enabling a captive portal that requires accepting the terms of use and setting a 2-hour session time limit.
Sample response. On the access point, I would enable the guest network feature, create a separate SSID such as Brightwater-Guest, set WPA3 (or at least WPA2) with a strong password, and disable guest access to the internal network so patrons reach only the internet. I would enable a captive portal where patrons must accept the terms of use and set a session limit of 2 hours. I would test it by connecting a device and confirming internet access but no access to 192.168.10.8.
(e) For policy L4, the library's new server will use the IPv6 address 2001:0DB8:00A0:0000:0000:0000:0000:0005. Write this address in compressed format, and explain one way IPv6 supports the library's growth. (2 pt; LO 4.6.A)
  • 1 point: Writes 2001:DB8:A0::5.
  • 1 point: Explains a valid benefit, such as 128-bit addressing (2^128 addresses) giving every device a globally unique, routable address so the library will not run out of addresses, or more efficient transmission from simplified headers and routing.
Sample response. The compressed address is 2001:DB8:A0::5: leading zeros are dropped (0DB8 → DB8, 00A0 → A0, 0005 → 5) and the four all-zero hextets become ::. IPv6 uses 128-bit addresses, so there are enough addresses for every new device the library adds to have its own globally unique address.
