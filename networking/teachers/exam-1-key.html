1D1.1.B3.ACPU use above 80–90% for a sustained period means the processor is overloaded, and one process is using 81% while not responding. Storage is 41% free (well above 10%), RAM is 58%, and the signal is 4 of 4 bars.Lesson 1.1
2B1.1.C3.BThe solution for high CPU use is to end the high-usage or unresponsive task. It is simple, free, and nondisruptive, so it is tried first. RAM and storage are not the problem, and the signal is already strong.Lesson 1.1
3C1.1.A3.BAfter a solution is implemented, it must be verified by confirming that the device functions normally. Hardware replacement is a last resort, AI output must be verified by hand, and starting over is only needed if verification fails.Lesson 1.1
4A1.2.A1.AWireless network passwords are case-sensitive, so the same letters with different capitalization are a different password. WPA3 networks accept passwords, an adapter that is off would not show the SSID list, and a hidden SSID does not reject a correct password.Lesson 1.2
5B1.2.B1.BA weak signal causes lag, and switching to a wired Ethernet connection when one is available removes the distance and wall interference problem. Closing tabs and deleting files help device performance, not signal strength; disconnecting one smart speaker frees very little bandwidth.Lesson 1.2
6D1.3.A2.AMalware can let attackers access files, monitor activity, take remote control of a device, and use it to spread to other systems. The other options describe events that malware on one computer does not cause.Lesson 1.3
7D1.3.B2.BThe emails are phishing. The matching controls are email filtering to block malicious messages and training users to recognize and report them. Privacy screens and screen positioning address shoulder surfing.Lesson 1.3
8C1.4.A2.CT7#qmV!r2pLx is 12 characters long, uses all four character sets, and contains no words or personal information. Rover1985! uses a pet name and a birth year, sunshine2009 is a dictionary word with only two sets, and PASSWORD!!!! is a common word.Lesson 1.4
9D1.4.B2.COn untrusted networks, users should connect through a secure network such as a VPN and make sure websites use HTTPS, which encrypts traffic. Automatic connection to open networks, password reuse, and disabled updates all increase risk.Lesson 1.4
10A1.4.C2.DWPA3 is the recommended encryption and WPS should be disabled. WPA2 is acceptable only as a minimum, and leaving WPS on weakens it. WEP and WPA are insecure regardless of key length or a hidden SSID.Lesson 1.4
11D2.1.A3.AAn unlit indicator light usually means a faulty port or cable or an unplugged cable. The other ports are working, which rules out an ISP outage; DHCP problems do not turn off a link light; and the computer is wired, not wireless.Lesson 2.1
12A2.2.A1.AA MAC address is a 48-bit identifier hardcoded into the NIC. The first half is the Organizationally Unique Identifier (OUI) of the manufacturer, and the second half identifies the device. It stays the same on every network. A 32-bit logical address and a network/host split describe IPv4 addresses, not MAC addresses.Lesson 2.2
13B2.2.B1.AThe mask 255.255.0.0 marks the first two octets as network bits, so the network ID is 172.20 and the last two octets (14.9) are host bits.Lesson 2.2
14D2.2.C1.AAddresses from 169.254.0.0 to 169.254.255.255 are Automatic Private IP Addressing (APIPA) addresses, which a host uses when the DHCP server is unavailable or misconfigured. Loopback is 127.0.0.1, and the office's private network is 192.168.10.0/24.Lesson 2.2
15C2.1.A3.AThe APIPA address and the empty default gateway show the desktop never received its IP configuration. The Internet light is green and other computers work (so the connection is up), the port light is flashing green (so the cable link is up), and the ping never left the desktop.Lesson 2.1
16A2.1.B3.BFor a device with an APIPA address, the matching solutions are disconnecting and reconnecting or releasing and renewing the IP address. Enabling the adapter and restarting is the fix for a loopback-only address; changing ports is for unlit or red lights; replacing the router is costly and unnecessary.Lesson 2.1
17A2.4.A1.BAI suggestions can be technically accurate yet unrealistic or overcomplicated for a SOHO network. Two 48-port switches and a rack for six devices cost eight times the budget and add management work a two-person business does not need.Lesson 2.4
18A2.4.B1.CA media server should have a wired connection for speed and reliability and a static IP address or hostname so other devices can always find it. A changing address breaks that, a guest network exposes it, and file-sharing software is required for it to serve files.Lesson 2.4
19C2.3.B1.BSTP is affordable, easy to install, supports up to 10 Gbps over 100 meters, and is the choice for environments with high EMI such as HVAC equipment and fluorescent lighting. UTP is fine for standard installations, fiber is immune to EMI but expensive and not common inside SOHO LANs, and coaxial is used mainly where it already exists.Lesson 2.3
20D2.3.C1.BStatic addressing fits devices that need a consistent address, such as servers, printers, remote services, and security systems. Mobile and temporary devices such as phones, laptops, tablets, and guest devices are better served by dynamic addressing.Lesson 2.3
21A2.5.A2.ADefault usernames and passwords are widely known, so anyone who reaches the router's login page could take control of it, change configurations, or disable security controls. The other choices are not effects of default credentials.Lesson 2.5
22C2.5.B2.BIsolating IoT devices on a separate segment from critical devices and data limits lateral movement. Static addresses and router placement do not restrict access, and WPS should be disabled because it weakens wireless security.Lesson 2.5
23A2.6.A2.AFirewalls limit the traffic allowed into or out of a network by evaluating IP addresses, ports, and protocols. Between zones, that keeps guests from reaching internal systems and stops malware or adversaries from moving laterally. Encryption is a wireless setting, not a firewall function.Lesson 2.6
24C2.6.B2.CGrouping by risk places often-vulnerable IoT devices such as thermostats, speakers, and smart TVs together. The office computer and NAS belong in the high-security segment, and the visitor's phone belongs on the guest wireless segment.Lesson 2.6
25C2.6.B2.CDevices can be grouped by performance needs to prioritize high-bandwidth or low-latency traffic, such as gaming consoles and media servers used for streaming. Risk-level and user-type grouping serve security and access control goals instead.Lesson 2.6
26B3.1.A1.BGaming consoles, desktops, or high-performance laptops are needed where high frame rates and low input latency are required. Tablets and smartphones suit mobile access to forms and dashboards, and basic laptops suit browser-based multitasking.Lesson 3.1
27B3.1.B1.B140 ÷ 40 = 3.5, and a partial WAP is not possible, so the hall needs 4 WAPs. Three WAPs would support only 120 devices.Lesson 3.1
28C3.1.B1.BEach switch has 24 − 1 = 23 ports for devices. Two switches give 46 ports, which is not enough; three switches give 69 ports, which covers 52 devices.Lesson 3.1
29D3.2.B1.BPhysical segmentation uses separate switches, routers, or cabling and should be used when maximum isolation is needed. It costs more, but the budget allows it. VLANs, SSIDs, and subnets are logical separations on shared hardware.Lesson 3.2
30B3.4.C2.C/27 means 27 network bits: 24 ones in the first three octets and 3 more in the fourth (11100000 = 224), so the mask is 255.255.255.224. /26 is .192, /28 is .240, and /29 is .248.Lesson 3.4
31B3.4.C2.C70 hosts plus the network and broadcast addresses need 72 addresses. The next power of 2 is 128 (7 host bits), which is a /25 with 126 usable hosts. A /26 has only 62 usable hosts.Lesson 3.4
32D3.3.B1.DWith a /27 mask, the computer's network is 192.168.40.64–95, and .101 falls outside it (the printer's /28 block is .96–.111). Because the network bits differ, the computer sends the data to its default gateway, which routes it to the other subnet.Lesson 3.3
33D3.5.C2.CACLs are checked from top to bottom, and the first matching rule is executed. Every staff and admin address is inside 10.20.0.0/16, so rule 101 denies the traffic before rules 102 and 103 are reached. HTTPS uses port 443, and the last rule applies only to traffic no earlier rule matched.Lesson 3.5
34D3.6.C3.BThe fix for this kind of problem is to reorder the ACL so specific allow rules sit above broad deny rules. A new rule 106 would never be reached, deleting rule 105 removes the final deny, and allowing port 443 from ALL is broader than needed and still blocked by rule 101.Lesson 3.6
35C3.5.C2.CRule 104 allows port 23, Telnet, which sends remote CLI sessions unencrypted. Administrators already have SSH on port 22 through rule 103, so Telnet should be closed.Lesson 3.5
36B3.2.C1.AMesh provides multiple paths, so communication continues when one connection fails. It needs more cabling and management, which a dedicated support team can handle. A star's central switch is a single point of failure, and bus and ring are avoided for their limited fault tolerance.Lesson 3.2
37C3.2.D1.BAI suggestions may leave out segments or meet only some requirements. Guests must be isolated from internal resources, but the plan has no guest segment, and a single SSID for everyone puts guests on the same wireless network as staff and students.Lesson 3.2
38D3.3.A1.CEvery IP address must be unique and within the network's range. .1 is already the router, .255 is the broadcast address, and 192.168.9.50 is on a different network than its gateway.Lesson 3.3
39C3.3.D1.DA correctly configured guest network gives internet access without access to internal resources, so the test must check both from a guest-connected device. The other choices check only that the network exists or test the internal network instead.Lesson 3.3
40C3.4.A2.AUnknown or unverified devices allowed onto a network may intercept traffic, spread malware, or bypass internal security controls. Controls include MAC filtering, setting unused ports to down, and DHCP reservations.Lesson 3.4
41B3.5.B2.BAn NGFW adds intrusion prevention and application-based filtering and suits high-risk zones. A stateless firewall uses fixed rules, a stateful firewall tracks and logs connections but does not filter by application, and a host-based firewall protects a single device.Lesson 3.5
42A3.6.A3.AGood documentation has a labeled diagram of wired and wireless connections, consistent device names and roles, the IP scheme with static devices noted, and segments with special firewall rules. It must be updated whenever something changes, and it should never expose passwords.Lesson 3.6
43B3.6.B3.AAn exhausted DHCP pool causes new devices to be assigned APIPA addresses. All 50 addresses are leased, devices already connected work, and only the newly arriving laptops fail.Lesson 3.6
44A4.1.A2.AIntegrity means data are accurate and unaltered, and unauthorized data changes are evidence of an integrity breach. Nothing shows the database was down, the vacation schedule is not the exposed data, and the log was not deleted (it is the evidence).Lesson 4.1
45D4.1.B3.AThe impact of a lost connection depends on timing and on the system affected. Outages during peak activity cause greater impacts, and the ordering system is essential to the business, while the guest Wi-Fi and training server are not.Lesson 4.1
46B4.4.C1.CEach line is a hop, and the destination is reached at hop 6. Response times are low through hop 3 and jump to about 200 ms at hop 4 (192.0.2.17), staying high afterward, so the delay starts there, beyond the school's gateway and its ISP's first routers.Lesson 4.4
47B4.4.A1.ANetwork address translation (NAT) lets devices with private addresses (10.1.x.x) communicate with external devices. The gateway replaces the source address with its single external address and reverses the translation for return traffic.Lesson 4.4
48D4.5.C2.AFrequent packet drops and errors suggest a technical problem, such as a failing cable or port, not necessarily an adversary. Unauthorized scans, repeated failed logins, and connections to known malicious addresses are threat indicators.Lesson 4.5
49C4.1.A2.ANetwork traffic showing exfiltration of sensitive data is evidence of a confidentiality breach. A 2.3 GB transfer from the records server to a listed malicious address after an attacker logged in fits that pattern. The log shows no outage or changed records.Lesson 4.1
50A4.5.B2.BAccount lockout after a set number of invalid attempts keeps an adversary from guessing passwords continuously. The 48 failed attempts would have triggered it long before the login succeeded.Lesson 4.5
51C4.2.A3.BDNS translates domain names into IP addresses. The laptop has working connectivity (the IP address works), so the name translation step is failing. ARP maps IP addresses to MAC addresses, DHCP assigns addresses, and ICMP is used by ping and traceroute.Lesson 4.2
52B4.2.B1.AThe network layer (layer 3) uses IP addresses and routers, and its PDU is the packet. Layer 2 uses MAC addresses and frames, layer 4 uses TCP and UDP segments, and layer 7 holds application data.Lesson 4.2
53A4.2.C3.BA bottom-up approach starts with physical connection checks (layer 1) and moves up through the network layer to the transport layer, where firewalls sit. Top-down begins with applications, and middle-out begins with network settings.Lesson 4.2
54C4.3.A1.Ccd .. moves one level toward the root, from /home/jlee/Documents to /home/jlee, and pwd prints the current working directory.Lesson 4.3
55D4.3.A1.CCLI commands follow the pattern command, options, arguments. ls is the program, -a is an option (flag) that also lists hidden files, and /etc is the argument naming the directory to list.Lesson 4.3
56A4.3.B1.DAn SFTP session is opened with sftp username@host, and get downloads a file from the remote device to the local device. put uploads, which is the wrong direction, and the username comes before the @.Lesson 4.3
57A4.4.B1.ARIP uses hop count as its metric and picks the path with the fewest routers, so Route X (3 hops) wins even though Route Y's links are faster. A protocol that weighs link speed and bandwidth, such as OSPF, might choose differently.Lesson 4.4
58B4.5.D2.CBasic VLAN configuration is create the VLAN, assign its ID, and assign ports. Devices join a VLAN through access ports configured with that VLAN ID. Trunk ports carry multiple VLANs between switches or routers, and a VLAN is not a gateway address.Lesson 4.5
59B4.6.A1.ALeading zeros are removed from each hextet (0DB8 → DB8, 0040 → 40, 0A01 → A01), and one run of all-zero hextets (the three in a row) is replaced by ::. The single zero hextet that remains is written as 0. :: can appear only once in an address, and an address that drops hextets without using :: is invalid.Lesson 4.6
60A4.6.B3.BRedundancy improves reliability: backup power through a UPS covers brief outages, and mobile or satellite internet covers primary ISP outages. The other choices improve security or organization, not availability during these failures.Lesson 4.6

Section II scoring guidelines: Cedar Valley Veterinary Clinic (11 points)

(a) Identify the rule in Source 2 that violates policy P2, and explain why. Then identify the rule in Source 2 that violates policy P3. (2 pt; LO 3.5.C, 2.6.A)
  • 1 point: Identifies rule 102 and explains that it allows Telnet (port 23), which sends remote CLI sessions, including credentials, unencrypted.
  • 1 point: Identifies rule 103, which allows all TCP traffic from the IoT segment (192.168.40.0/24) into the records server segment.
Sample response. Rule 102 violates P2 because it allows Telnet on port 23, an unencrypted remote CLI protocol, so administrator credentials and commands could be read if captured. Rule 103 violates P3 because it allows every TCP port from the IoT segment 192.168.40.0/24 into the records server segment.
(b) Using Source 3, identify two different indicators of a security threat. Then identify which part of the CIA triad was compromised and cite the specific evidence that supports your answer. (3 pt; LO 4.5.C, 4.1.A)
  • 1 point: Identifies one threat indicator from the log, such as 260 repeated failed SSH logins from 192.168.40.14 or a login outside business hours (01:40 on Sunday).
  • 1 point: Identifies a second, different indicator, such as the unauthorized port scan of the staff segment at 01:51 or the successful login from a camera, a device that has no reason to log in to the server.
  • 1 point: Identifies integrity as compromised, citing the 1,820 vaccination_date records modified at 01:44 right after the unauthorized login (and the wrong due dates staff saw on Monday).
Sample response. One indicator is the 260 failed SSH logins for root from 192.168.40.14 at 01:12, which shows password guessing. A second is the port scan of the staff segment from the same address at 01:51. Integrity was compromised: four minutes after kennel-cam-3 logged in as vetadmin, 1,820 vaccination_date records were modified, which is why the front desk sees wrong due dates.
(c) Write a revised ACL for traffic entering the records server segment, using the same format as Source 2. Your ACL must allow staff to reach the records application over HTTPS, allow encrypted remote administration only from the IT admin workstation, and follow policies P1, P2, and P3. (3 pt; LO 3.5.C, 3.4.B)
  • 1 point: Includes an ALLOW rule for TCP 443 from 192.168.10.0/24 (staff).
  • 1 point: Includes an ALLOW rule for TCP 22 (SSH) whose source is only 192.168.10.20 (or 192.168.10.20/32), and includes no rule allowing port 23 or traffic from 192.168.40.0/24.
  • 1 point: Ends with a DENY rule for all remaining traffic, placed below every ALLOW rule so the allow rules are reached first (deny-by-default).
Sample response. 
101 ALLOW inbound TCP 22  192.168.10.20
102 ALLOW inbound TCP 443 192.168.10.0/24
103 DENY  inbound ALL ALL ALL
The SSH rule is limited to the one admin workstation (least privilege), Telnet and IoT access are gone, and the final rule denies everything else because ACLs are read from top to bottom.
(d) The clinic will add 9 exam-room tablets on their own subnet, taken from the start of the network 192.168.50.0/24. Determine the smallest appropriate subnet in CIDR notation and the range of usable host addresses. (1 pt; LO 3.4.C)
  • 1 point: Determines /28 (255.255.255.240) with a usable host range of 192.168.50.1 to 192.168.50.14. Both are required for the point.
Sample response. 9 tablets + 2 reserved addresses = 11, and the next power of 2 is 16 (4 host bits), so the subnet is 192.168.50.0/28. The usable range is 192.168.50.1 to 192.168.50.14 (.0 is the network address and .15 is the broadcast address).
(e) Recommend two security controls, other than changing the firewall rules, that would reduce the chance of a similar incident. For each control, justify your recommendation with specific evidence from the sources. (2 pt; LO 2.5.B, 4.5.B)
  • 1 point: Recommends one appropriate control with justification tied to evidence, such as changing the cameras' default passwords (Source 1 says they were never changed; policy P5).
  • 1 point: Recommends a second, different control with justification tied to evidence, such as updating the 2019 camera firmware, enforcing account lockout after 5 failures (Source 3 shows 260 attempts; policy P4), or monitoring and alerting on after-hours logins.
Sample response. First, change the default administrator passwords on all kennel cameras, because Source 1 shows they were never changed, which breaks P5 and likely let an attacker take over kennel-cam-3. Second, enforce account lockout after 5 failed attempts on the records server: the log shows 260 failed SSH logins with no lockout, so P4 is not being enforced, and lockout would have stopped the guessing long before the 01:40 login.
